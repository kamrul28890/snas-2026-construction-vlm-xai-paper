\title{ConstructionSafety-FaithBench: Counterfactual Evaluation of Visual-Evidence Faithfulness in Vision-Language Safety Inspection}
\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{abstract}
Construction-safety vision-language models are often evaluated by final answers or plausible rationales, but neither shows whether a model used the rule-relevant visual evidence. We present ConstructionSafety-FaithBench, a compact benchmark and counterfactual audit pipeline for construction-safety VLMs. Each example pairs a site image with a normalized safety rule, an answer label, and localizable visual evidence. The implemented artifact includes 163 pilot image-rule pairs, 588 scale-up candidates, four safety-rule families, normalized model-output schemas, deterministic baselines, a Florence-2 grounding adapter, 948 targeted/matched-random occlusion runs, and a 120-row final audit-label layer. On the audit set, the current Florence grounding adapter reaches only 18.3\% answer accuracy, while a metadata-assisted bootstrap reaches 78.3\%, showing that the prioritized audit split exposes substantial model weakness hidden by scaffold-level validation. Targeted evidence occlusion changes Florence answers 39.2\% of the time versus 9.2\% for matched random masks, with a paired answer-flip gap of 30.0 points. The current final-label layer combines two independent AI-generated audit passes with returned adjudication for all 12 disagreements; we therefore report it with explicit provenance rather than as unqualified human ground truth.
\end{abstract}
