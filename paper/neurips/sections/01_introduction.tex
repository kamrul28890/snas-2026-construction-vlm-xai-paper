\section{Introduction}

Vision-language models (VLMs) could help screen construction imagery for safety-rule violations, but answer accuracy alone is too weak for this setting. A model may answer ``violation'' while grounding its decision on background scaffolding instead of a missing hard hat, or it may keep the same answer after the rule-relevant worker is masked. In safety inspection, this distinction matters: explanations are useful only when the answer, evidence, and intervention behavior agree.

ConstructionSafety-FaithBench operationalizes this distinction as a compact, reproducible audit suite. Each row binds an image, a natural-language safety question, a normalized rule, an answer label, and evidence boxes. Models must output both an answer and evidence regions. We then compare ordinary answer scores with evidence overlap and counterfactual sensitivity under targeted and same-size random masks.

The current implementation is intentionally small but end-to-end. It includes a 163-row pilot manifest, a 588-row scale-up candidate split, a 120-row prioritized audit set, deterministic baselines, a local Florence-2 grounding adapter, and a completed targeted-versus-random occlusion study. The final audit-label layer combines 108 A/B consensus rows with 12 returned adjudication decisions. Because the two audit passes were AI-generated, we preserve the provenance explicitly and avoid claiming unqualified human ground truth.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
This repository contributes: (i) a normalized four-rule construction-safety benchmark schema; (ii) pilot, scale-up, audit, intervention, and final-label artifacts with validation scripts; (iii) a model-output and scoring interface that separates answer correctness, evidence overlap, and counterfactual faithfulness; (iv) a 948-run Florence-2 occlusion study showing larger changes under rule-evidence masking than matched random masking; and (v) a compact paper/reproducibility package with generated figures, tables, checks, and SHA-256 artifact manifests.
