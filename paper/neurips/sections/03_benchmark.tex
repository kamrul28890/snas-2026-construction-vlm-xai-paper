\section{Benchmark}

Each benchmark example consists of an image identifier, source split, normalized safety rule, natural-language question, answer label, evidence metadata, and provenance fields. The four implemented rule families are hard-hat/PPE compliance, harness/fall protection, guardrail/edge protection, and struck-by/equipment proximity. The benchmark stores image identifiers and derived metadata, but does not redistribute raw source images in the repository.

The pilot manifest contains 163 image-rule pairs from the ConstructionSite test split. The scale-up candidate manifest contains 588 test-split image-rule pairs selected from source metadata. Table~\ref{tab:scaleup-counts} shows the scale-up class counts; the split remains imbalanced because struck-by and fall-hazard source cases are scarce under the current priority-labeling policy.

\paragraph{Annotation layers.}
We maintain three label layers. First, model-assisted bootstrap annotations are generated from source metadata, captions, rule-violation records, and available boxes. They are useful for scaling and triage but are not ground truth. Second, \texttt{human\_audit\_batch\_001} is a balanced 120-row audit queue with 30 rows per rule, prioritized for Florence/bootstrap disagreement and weak evidence overlap. Third, the final audit-label layer resolves the audit queue: 108 rows use A/B answer agreement from two independent AI-generated passes, and the 12 A/B disagreements use the returned adjudication package. This produces 83 violation, 31 compliant, and 6 uncertain labels. We report this layer as adjudicated audit labels with AI-pass provenance.

\paragraph{Architecture.}
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the complete artifact flow. Manifests and annotations feed normalized model adapters. Model outputs are scored against either bootstrap labels or final audit labels. Intervention specs reuse the same evidence boxes to compare targeted masks with same-size random controls. Every generated table is reproducible from checked CSV/JSONL files and is covered by lightweight validation.
