\section{Results}

\paragraph{Final audit-label layer.}
The 120-row audit set is intentionally difficult: it over-samples Florence/bootstrap disagreements and weak evidence overlap. Figure~\ref{fig:audit-dashboard} summarizes the label distribution and answer scores. The final labels contain 83 violation, 31 compliant, and 6 uncertain rows. Florence grounding reaches only 18.3\% accuracy and 12.5\% macro-F1, while the metadata-assisted bootstrap reaches 78.3\% accuracy and 50.5\% macro-F1. This gap is useful: the bootstrap layer remains a scaling aid, but the audit layer exposes visually grounded failure modes that bootstrap validation alone would miss.

\paragraph{Counterfactual faithfulness.}
Table~\ref{tab:pilot-interventions} summarizes the frozen pilot intervention specifications. Figure~\ref{fig:intervention-effects} and Table~\ref{tab:pilot-intervention-effects} show that targeted rule-evidence masks affect Florence much more than matched random masks. Targeted occlusion flips answers in 39.2\% of rows versus 9.2\% for random controls; the paired answer-flip gap is 30.0 points with a bootstrap 95\% interval of 22.3 to 37.7 points. Targeted masks also cause much larger centroid drift, indicating evidence relocation rather than only answer instability.

\paragraph{Scale-up diagnostics.}
Table~\ref{tab:scaleup-baselines} reports adapters on the 588-row scale-up candidate manifest against bootstrap annotations. The caption-keyword baseline is a leakage diagnostic; its poor performance indicates that this simple caption-only heuristic cannot solve the split. Figure~\ref{fig:rule-slices} shows that Florence usually emits some evidence, yet answer accuracy remains weak, especially for PPE hard-hat and struck-by equipment rules. This separation between evidence presence and answer correctness is the central empirical reason for reporting answer, grounding, and counterfactual metrics separately.
